Step 2 · The CPO’s question, answered

How far to consolidate — and how to get there

The test case every leader named

“I can’t see which products a customer has, what they’re worth, or where the upsell is.” The deliverable everyone wants: one always-current view per account — products subscribed, ARR/ACV per product, renewal dates, and the white-space gap. Same report, two structures:
Consolidated · one object model
~3–5 weeks
Configure + migrate. Products, native Quotes & the Subscriptions object ship natively — one revenue definition, zero integrations to reconcile. The long pole is data migration, not engineering.
Salesforce + Gainsight + Marketo · three systems
~4–6 months
  • Definition tax — first fix “ARR computes ACV” across 15 overlapping Revenue/ARR fields (47% of Opp fields are stakeholder-critical & wired to flows/Apex).
  • Build tax — build opportunity-products + a subscription model + backfill (manual for months to start).
  • Reconciliation tax — subscription truth split Gainsight ↔ Salesforce, reconciled every month, forever.
~4 weeks vs. ~4–5 months — to the exact same report. The difference isn’t effort, it’s structure: a consolidated model lets you migrate & configure; the three-system world makes you manufacture a revenue definition, build the subscription model by hand, then reconcile it every month. Caveat: assumes the ARR/ACV fix is a prerequisite (it is, for trustworthy numbers).

Why after consolidation — not before

Build it first (Option 4)

The warehouse has to reconcile all ~48 sources + 3 systems from day one — the heaviest build, ~2 FTE to run, needed sooner, and the most expensive. You pay to centralize data around the fragmentation instead of removing it.

Heaviest · ~2 FTE · sooner
Consolidate first (Options 1 → 2)

Each consolidation step removes sources the warehouse would otherwise reconcile. By the time you build it, the layer is mostly product/call telemetry joining a clean GTM record — lighter, later, cheaper.

Lighter · later · cheaper

When to bring in the CTO

It becomes a CTO conversation — not a RevOps one — at these trigger points. Any one of them is the signal to pull the CTO in.

1
Scope crosses beyond RevOps. The need shifts from GTM reporting to org-wide BI & a governed data model — finance, product and exec dashboards drinking from one source.
2
After Phase 1–2 consolidation. Once the source list is smaller, the warehouse scope is defensible and the build is sized right — not a moving target.
3
Product/call telemetry must join GTM data. This is Adri’s #2 (churn & expansion signals) and Sam’s usage/overage — it needs raw telemetry at scale, i.e., a lake, which is the CTO’s domain.
4
Governed self-serve is the ask. When the ~290 ad-hoc dashboards become a trust/security problem, you need a catalog, semantic layer and ownership — an architecture decision, not a report.

What it actually is

A unified data / context layer — sized to scale:

🗄️ Warehouse

Snowflake / BigQuery for structured GTM + finance data and modeled reporting.

🌊 Data lake

For raw / unstructured product & call telemetry and AI use cases (schema-on-read). Together with the warehouse = a lakehouse at true scale.

🔁 Pipes + governance

ETL / reverse-ETL, a semantic layer, and a catalog + ownership so every team trusts the same numbers.
